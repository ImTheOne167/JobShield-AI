Common Job Scam Warning Signs

Watch out for these red flags when evaluating a job opportunity.

Requests for Money

Genuine employers normally do not ask candidates to pay money for a job, interview or recruitment process.

Suspicious Email Addresses

Be careful when recruiters use unofficial email addresses or free email services instead of a company domain.

Unrealistic Salary

Extremely high salaries for entry-level positions can be a warning sign, especially when combined with other suspicious details.

No Proper Interview

Be cautious if someone offers you a job immediately without a reasonable interview or selection process.

Fake Offer Letters

Scammers may send professional-looking offer letters containing fake company information, unusual email addresses or requests for payment.

WhatsApp or Telegram Recruitment

Be cautious when an unknown recruiter conducts the entire recruitment process through messaging apps without official company communication.

Requests for Bank or OTP Details

Never share OTPs, passwords, PINs or unnecessary banking information with recruiters or unknown individuals.

Pressure and Urgency

Scammers may pressure candidates to pay money or accept an offer immediately without giving them enough time to verify the opportunity.

Stay Safe

Research the Company

Check the company's official website, online presence and contact details.

Check Website URLs

Look carefully for unusual domains, spelling mistakes and suspicious links.

Protect Personal Information

Never share sensitive financial or personal information with unverified recruiters.

Verify the Recruiter

Use official company contact details to confirm that the recruiter actually works for the organization.

Report Suspicious Offers

If you identify a potential scam, avoid further communication and report it through appropriate channels.

When in doubt, verify the job and recruiter through official company channels before taking any action.
